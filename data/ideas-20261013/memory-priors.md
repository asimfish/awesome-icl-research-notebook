# Memory and inference mechanism audit

As of 2026-10-13. Scope: C11, C12, C15, C21, C35 only; no ranking, candidate edits, experiments, or robot-validation claim. Read the research brief, the five supplied candidate blocks, and existing RAD/CML/VE digests. New sources below have descriptive keys in the companion JSON; they are **not registered local numeric IDs**. Existing registry IDs remain 1–15.

Eight targeted queries and four new primary bodies were used. Metadata/abstract inspection is explicitly weaker than body evidence. No quantitative paper results are used to select candidates.

## Four load-bearing bodies

**CPSR2014:** Hamilton, Milani Fard and Pineau, *Efficient Learning and Planning with Compressed Predictive States*, arXiv:1312.0286v2, 2014-07-20; initial submission 2013-12-01. Read §§2.2–2.3, 3.2, 4.1–4.2, 7.1.5. The direct mechanism is recursive prediction from observable action/observation histories, including state update Eq.11 and compressed moment/SVD learning Eqs.31–38. Below-system-rank compression is approximate; the theory distinguishes test compression from history compression. [Primary body](https://arxiv.org/html/1312.0286).

**SelfPredictive2024:** Ni et al., *Bridging State and History Representations: Understanding Self-Predictive RL*, arXiv:2401.08898v3, 2024-04-21; initial submission 2024-01-17, ICLR 2024. Read §§2–4.3, Appendix A.2–A.3. A next-latent prediction auxiliary loss is already a concrete operation for learning history representations. Exact abstraction conditions and practical sampled objectives must be distinguished; stochasticity matters. [Primary body](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08898v3).

**SCMC2021:** Beronov, Weilbach, Wood and Campbell, *Sequential core-set Monte Carlo*, PMLR 161:2165–2175, 2021 (conference 2021-07-27–30; precise publication day not asserted). Read §§2–3.2, Algorithm 1, Table 1. Online sparse weighted likelihood memory is combined with ESS resampling and approximate rejuvenation. The authors explicitly defer online projection-filter error bounds. [Primary body](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v161/beronov21a/beronov21a.pdf).

**IPSMC2014:** Jun and Bouchard-Côté, *Memory (and Time) Efficient Sequential Monte Carlo*, PMLR 32(2):514–522, 2014-06-18 in the proceedings metadata. Read §§2–3.5, Proposition 1/Eq.1 and Proposition 2. A two-pass particle stream stores survivors and chooses implicit population size using expected distinct survivors. The memory target is an expectation, with realized count bounded by the resample population size. [Primary body](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/jun14.pdf).

## Candidate comparisons

**C15 — response-stratified particle allocation.** Suffix moves, resample-move, and allocation under a memory budget are existing operations. The remaining proposed change is the allocation criterion: a catalogue of legally supportable future feedback responses. An additional direct proximity signal is Pacheco et al., *Preserving Modes and Messages via Diverse Particle Selection*, PMLR 32(2):1152–1160, 2014: the primary abstract describes selecting hypotheses to minimize errors in subsequent max-product message updates. This occupies the broad intuition of preserving diversity for later inference operations, although its MAP/message setting differs from a causal weighted filter; its body was not read. [Primary metadata/abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v32/pacheco14.html).

Reader-derived mass check: for cluster mass \(W_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i\), allocate \(n_g\ge1\), draw within cluster with probability \(w_i/W_g\), and assign each child weight \(W_g/n_g\). Conditional on the current population and allocation, this preserves its empirical measure **in expectation**. It does not guarantee exact posterior filtering or better future decisions. Keeping one deterministic representative per cluster needs a separate approximation argument. More positive-mass response clusters than particles makes universal cluster preservation impossible. Fingerprints must include evidence likelihood and post-update query prediction; current action labels alone do not establish future-update equivalence. A suffix move cannot recover a discarded prefix unless its kernel can change that prefix.

Contestable delta: whether a specified, computably bounded response-allocation objective improves prefeedback new-goal decisions at matched total likelihood calls, stored bytes, replay/move costs and catalogue construction. Strong controls must include ordinary resample-move and adaptive particle allocation; a decision-sensitive diversity comparator is necessary. Auxiliary/lookahead particle-filter allocation remains an **unresolved body comparison**, not a claimed absence of prior work.

**C21 — product-query insertion.** Existing registered VE defines equivalence relative to selected Bellman policies/functions; adding a distinguishing conjunction query expands that set. CPSR supplies a recursive prediction reference. The annotation's Seipp–Helmert paper, *Counterexample-Guided Cartesian Abstraction Refinement*, DOI 10.1609/icaps.v23i1.13605, 2013-06-02, has a primary abstract describing repeated abstract-plan checking and refinement; no body was read here. [Primary metadata/abstract](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICAPS/article/view/13605).

Reader inference: merely finding models that agree on retained queries but disagree on a conjunction, then adding that conjunction, is adaptive basis expansion/refinement. The possible narrower difference is a specific low-cost model-pair search and receipt-indexed update which avoids full planner/replay cost while retaining uncertainty. The candidate currently does not specify that cost advantage. Counterexample access cannot silently use true symbolic relations or unseen-goal outcomes. Compare identical receipt sets against adaptive refinement and expanding multi-goal VE; measure pair search and selective-replay bookkeeping.

**C11 — update-closed history partition.** CPSR and recurrent predictive abstractions already retain distinctions for future observation-conditioned updates. Its table-based construction differs from spectral projection or neural auxiliary learning, but finite recorded continuations give only empirical closure on those edges. Reader inference: backward propagation of successor distinctions is a partition-refinement operation; no theorem about unseen continuations follows from it. The potential delta is the particular bounded compiler and its memory/coverage cost, not update-sufficient memory itself. Compare equal-byte recurrent predictive states and multi-goal memory, including counterexample/refinement cost. The annotation's POMDP CEGAR paper is metadata/abstract verified, not a body-established exact duplicate. [Primary metadata](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.06209).

**C35 — post-correction decision distillation.** Training next-latent prediction and maintaining recursively updateable predictive states are already explicit mechanisms. The possible objective change is supervising the downstream decision after each legally supported next-event branch, rather than only matching the current action or an averaged latent target. Reader inference: that can be a decision-targeted training choice under a limited query budget; this audit does not establish a new sufficient-statistics principle. Its same-catalogue control must include multi-goal RAD plus next-state/observation prediction and matched branch-conditioned decision training. Neither current-action agreement nor a low auxiliary latent loss proves future correction transfer.

**C12 — checkpoint commitment and replay.** The OpenReview annotation id `TntCH2maP7` was challenge-blocked; direct API access failed. Its precise primary title/date and bounded-delay mechanism are therefore **unverified** in this audit. A secondary result suggests the title *Model-Based Reinforcement Learning under Random Observation Delays*, but it is not treated as verified evidence. [Unretrieved primary note](https://openreview.net/forum?id=TntCH2maP7).

The Bobu et al. annotation, *Learning under Misspecified Objective Spaces*, PMLR 87:796–805, 2018, is metadata/abstract verified: relevance-sensitive conservative interpretation of physical human corrections is already a prior. This inspection does not establish rollback or confidence-based commitment. [Primary metadata/abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v87/bobu18a.html). SCMC also makes evidence-dependent online compression an explicit operation, but not a checkpoint/pending-event algorithm.

Reader inference: the possible C12 delta is an evidence-sensitive **commit/evict rule**, not Bayesian revision or replay. Statistical ambiguity that becomes identifiable later differs from a measurement arriving out of order. A checkpoint cannot exactly reinterpret an already compressed event after eviction unless sufficient event-level information remains. The rule must define revision risk, pending storage, replay work and irreversible eviction. Equal-byte fixed-window replay and exact full-history filtering remain decisive controls. An exact confidence-commit prior cannot be ruled out from this bounded search.

## Remaining evidence boundaries

The Hamilton 2013 annotation was identity verified as the earlier uncontrolled-dynamics CPSR paper; its body was not separately read. The controlled 2014 extension above is the load-bearing comparison. [Primary metadata](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v28/hamilton13.html).

The search found *Annealed Flow Transport Monte Carlo*, Arbel, Matthews and Doucet, PMLR 139:318–330, 2021. Its primary abstract establishes learned sequential normalizing-flow transport alongside SMC, but provides no body-level evidence about C15's response-cluster criterion. [Primary metadata/abstract](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/arbel21a.html).

No paper here establishes reusable relation learning from the project's robot correction stream. No candidate's failure premise was experimentally verified. The comparison leaves precise construction, allocation and commitment hypotheses to specify and test; it supplies no novelty certificate.
